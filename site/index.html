SAYA

сая: Kazakh for shade, as in саябақ, a park

A route planner for Astana's hot summers. For a person with disabilities it finds a way they can actually get through, works out how much of it is in the sun, and decides whether the shadier detour is worth it today. Anyone else picks the fastest route or the one with most shade.

The trip

The shortest way is not always the one to take

A person with disabilities goes from QAIRU, the Qazaq AI Research University at Alem.ai, to the main entrance of Nazarbayev University on 15 July at 16:00. The feels-like temperature is 31 °C. The direct way is 1455 m, but its pavement on Nurlan Balgymbayev Street is blocked by parked cars (a simulated resident report), so A* has to go around.

Sample run of python main.py, with the weather reading cached in the repo.

RouteLengthIn sunMinutesMinutes in sun
Fastest1741 m91%32.229.3
Most shade1765 m88%32.728.7

Both routes avoid the blocked pavement. The shadier one costs 25 m and half a minute more and saves only 0.6 minutes in the sun, so SAYA takes the fastest barrier-free route. On the longer trip to the Botanical Garden the shade wins.

How it decides

Three course algorithms, each feeding the next

A* produces two routes. Bayes turns a noisy forecast into the chance of dangerous heat. The MDP weighs both and picks one. The figures below run the same rules on small examples; drag across them, or press play.

OSM + shade → 1 · A* two routes → 2 · Bayes P(dangerous heat) → 3 · MDP fastest or most shade

1 · Search

A* finds two routes the person can pass

Every edge of the real OpenStreetMap walk network gets a cost. For a person with disabilities, steps, kerbs without a ramp, broken ramps and narrow or steep paths are impassable; roads without a sidewalk, paving stones and crossings without a traffic light cost extra. For a person without disabilities only a construction site blocks the way. Sun on the edge costs extra too, scaled by the weight w.

cost = length × access penalty
     × (1 + w × sun_fraction) + crossing penalty

With w = 0 A* returns the fastest route this person can pass; with w = 3 a metre in the sun counts as four, and it returns the one with most shade. The heuristic is the straight-line distance to the goal. No edge costs less than its own length, so the heuristic never overestimates and A* stays optimal.

input
5125 walk nodes, 15084 edges, shade measured on each
output
two paths with length, share in sun and minutes

2 · Inference

Bayes asks whether it is dangerously hot right now

The true state, Dangerous or Safe, is hidden. SAYA sees one noisy sensor: the Open-Meteo feels-like temperature, put in a bucket. The prior comes from the month: 0.30 for July.

P(reading | state)cool <24 °Cwarmhot ≥30 °C
Dangerous0.050.250.70
Safe0.550.350.10
 
P(Dangerous | hot) = 0.70 · 0.30
                   / (0.70 · 0.30 + 0.10 · 0.70)
                   = 0.21 / 0.28 = 0.75
input
prior 0.30 and a reading of 31.0 °C feels-like, so "hot"
output
P(dangerous heat) = 0.75

3 · Decision

An MDP picks the route

The person is Comfortable or HeatStressed; the actions are the two A* routes. Each minute in direct sun is a small chance of heat stress: 3% when the heat is dangerous, 0.2% when it is safe, times the person's vulnerability (1.5 for a person with disabilities), averaged over the Bayes belief. The reward is minus the walking minutes, and ending heat-stressed costs 60.

Q(s, a) = R(s, a) + γ · Σ P(s' | s, a) · V₀(s')
γ = 0.9, V₀(Comfortable) = 0, V₀(HeatStressed) = −60
Fastest
Q = −63.37, the higher, so take the fastest route
Most shade
Q = −63.52

One Bellman update, then argmax over Q(Comfortable, a). For a person with disabilities the answer is the route they get, with no choice to make. A person without disabilities sees it as a suggestion and switches between fastest and most shade.

The map

Plan your own trip on a 3D map

  • Buildings drawn from the shadow model. Every OSM building and building part stands at its real height, on columns where it has them, with its flat, dome or pyramid roof. Hover one to see its height and where that number comes from.
  • Any time of day. Drag the timeline from 05:00 to 22:00 or press play. The shade on the ground turns and stretches, and the route is recomputed. After sunset the windows light up.
  • Your own trip, to the door. Search a place by name, tap a quick pick, or click the map, then drag either marker. A trip to a building ends at its entrance: the main one, or the one facing the other end of the trip.
  • Two profiles. A person with disabilities gets one barrier-free route and the list of barriers it goes around. A person without disabilities switches between the fastest route and the one with most shade, and SAYA marks the one it suggests.
  • Barriers and map fixes on the map. Steps, kerbs without a ramp, a broken ramp, a blocked pavement and a steep ramp, each with an icon. A wrench marks a crossing that OpenStreetMap draws in pieces and SAYA joins by hand.
  • The decision first, then the reasons. One card says which route to take and why. "How SAYA decided" opens the A* table, the Bayes calculation and the MDP Q-table, the same numbers the console prints.

The browser only draws. Shadows, A*, Bayes and the MDP all run in Python behind FastAPI; the map is MapLibre GL on free OpenFreeMap tiles, with three.js for the buildings. On a phone the planner becomes a bottom sheet.

Assumptions

What is measured and what is assumed

Every number lives in config.py and is a labelled assumption, not a measurement. Change one and rerun main.py to see the effect.

  • Building heights come from the OSM height tag (14 pieces) or from levels × 3.2 m (469). The other 551 use a default: 9 m, or less for small types such as garages and sheds.
  • Building parts follow OSM's Simple 3D Buildings scheme, each with its own base height and roof. The Nur Alem sphere is set to a sphere by hand in data/landmarks.json.
  • Trees come from OSM: 34 single trees, 66 tree rows and 25 woods. Parks probably have more trees than are mapped, so real shade is likely higher than computed.
  • Five of the seven barrier reports in data/barriers.json are simulated for the demo. The two raised kerbs come from OSM (kerb=raised), and steps drawn in OSM count as well.
  • Map fixes and the Nazarbayev University main entrance in data/map_fixes.json are set by hand from the OSM geometry, each with a note. The crossing in front of NU is assumed to have a traffic light. Without the fixes, the direct way from QAIRU to NU went 3.8 km around the campus; with them it is 1.5 km.
  • The monthly priors, the likelihood table, the heat-stress rates, the vulnerability multipliers and the −60 penalty are illustrative values. Shadows come from buildings and trees only; terrain, awnings and bus shelters are ignored.

Run it

Two minutes on a laptop, offline

Python 3.10 or newer. The map data and the weather readings are saved in the repo, so the demo runs without a network.

git clone https://github.com/Akanov-Kanta/saya && cd saya
python3 -m venv .venv && source .venv/bin/activate && pip install -r requirements.txt
python server.py # 3D map at http://127.0.0.1:8765
python main.py # console version, every step printed
python -m pytest # 30 tests
